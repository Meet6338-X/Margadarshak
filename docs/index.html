The gap

The information is public. The path to it is not.

A student in a village often has to choose a stream, a course and a scholarship from WhatsApp forwards, a neighbour's advice and five different government portals. One stale link or missed deadline can cost a family a year.

  • 01

    Scattered by design

    Central schemes, state portals, exam boards and apprenticeships each live somewhere else. The eligibility fine print is buried in PDFs.

  • 02

    English by default

    The students who need guidance most are the least served by English-first answers, jargon and forms written for adults.

  • 03

    Expensive to get wrong

    A forwarded message expires. A link changes. Families have no easy way to tell which page is official — or whether they qualify.

The idea

It would rather say “I don't know” than invent a scheme.

A language model can sound certain about anything. For money, deadlines and eligibility, sounding certain is not the same as being right. So the work is split into three jobs that cannot borrow each other's authority.

01

Rules decide

Every opportunity carries machine-checkable rules: category, income band, class range, state, gender, first-generation status. A pure Dart engine evaluates them against the student's profile. No model call. No network call. No randomness.

02

Sources prove

Each record must have an official URL and a last-verified date. A data-pack test fails the build if either is missing. If an apply page was not verified, the app sends the student to the official source instead of inventing a link.

03

The AI explains

The model can translate, summarise and personalise the explanation. It never decides who qualifies. The verdict and the reason always come from the rule engine and the verified record.

The refusal

“When nothing matches, the list is empty. That is the product working.”

No “you may be eligible”, no invented deadline, no plausible-looking amount. If the profile is missing a field, the app asks for it. If a rule fails, it shows the rule. A student can check every answer against the source.

How it works

From four questions to a next step that holds up.

The student answers only what the eligibility rules need. No name, no phone number, no password to start browsing.

  1. 01

    Build a profile

    Class, state and district, category and income band, interests. Four steps, editable later.

  2. 02

    Discover careers

    Interest tags are scored against 22 career paths. Non-STEM and local, traditional skills are never penalised.

  3. 03

    Check every rule

    Each opportunity is evaluated separately. A failed blocking rule means not eligible; a missing field means a question, not a guess.

  4. 04

    Leave with a next step

    The official link, the deadline, the documents to collect and the course or class that comes next.

The product

Six screens. One clear path.

Designed for a low-cost Android phone, a weak connection and a first-time smartphone user. Every screen works offline.

Screenshots from the release build · Android · English UI shown; Marathi and Hindi ship as equals.

Facilitator mode

Teachers and mentors run classes, share modules and follow a roster — so one guide can serve a whole room.

Deadlines that speak plainly

“57 days left”, not a date to decode. Opening and closing windows come from the record, not a guess.

Documents, demystified

What to collect, where to get it, and what is mandatory — attached to the opportunity that needs it.

Voice, offline

On-device text-to-speech reads the daily clips aloud. The verified catalogue ships inside the app.

Who it is for

Built for the student the system reaches last.

She may be the first in her family to finish school. She is choosing a stream this year, on a phone the family already owns, with a connection that comes and goes. The advice she gets should be checkable, in her language, and honest about what she does not qualify for.

Margadarshak is built for her — and for the teacher who guides a whole classroom of her.

  • Class 9 to graduation
  • ITI, diploma and degree routes
  • Teachers, mentors and facilitators
